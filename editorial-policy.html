Editorial Policy

How we research, write, review and correct the lessons on Phonetics Halo.

Last updated: September 30, 2026

Learners rely on us for accurate information about how English sounds. This page explains the standards we follow when creating and maintaining lessons on Phonetics Halo.

Research and sources

Our explanations follow established descriptions of English phonetics used in linguistics and English language teaching, including the conventions of the International Phonetic Alphabet. Where scholars and dictionaries use different symbols for the same sound, we choose the most widely used form and mention alternatives when helpful.

Writing standards

  • We explain technical terms in plain language the first time they appear.
  • Each lesson focuses on a clear, practical goal for learners.
  • Examples use common, everyday words wherever possible.
  • We describe accent differences respectfully and never present one accent as superior.

Testing with learners

Before publishing an exercise, we try it with learners from different language backgrounds and revise any instructions that cause confusion. We also check that every example word actually contains the target sound in General American English.

Corrections

If you spot an error, please tell us through our contact page. We review every report, correct confirmed mistakes promptly and update the page date for significant changes.

Independence

Phonetics Halo is free to use and may be supported by display advertising. Advertisers have no influence over our lessons, topics or recommendations. Any sponsored content in the future would be clearly labelled.

Images and accessibility

We use photographs from Unsplash under the Unsplash License, chosen to illustrate learning and speaking honestly. Every image has descriptive alternative text, and we aim to keep the website readable and usable on all devices.
