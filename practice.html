Pronunciation practice exercises

Minimal pairs, tongue twisters, a shadowing routine and a stress drill. Use the play buttons to hear each word, then say it yourself.

Before you start

How to get the most from practice

  1. Warm up. Yawn, stretch your lips wide, then round them tightly. Loose muscles move more precisely.
  2. Listen before speaking. Play a word two or three times before you try it.
  3. Go slow, then speed up. Accuracy first. Speed comes naturally with repetition.
  4. Record yourself at the start of the week and at the end so you can hear your progress.
  5. Keep it short. Ten focused minutes a day works better than an hour once a week.

Exercise 2

Tongue twisters

Say each one slowly three times, then at normal speed three times. Stop as soon as the sounds blur and slow down again.

Red lorry, yellow lorry.

Focus: /r/ and /l/ switching

Three free throws.

Focus: /θ/ against /f/ and /r/

She sells seashells by the seashore.

Focus: /ʃ/ and /s/

Which witch is which?

Focus: /w/ and /tʃ/

A proper copper coffee pot.

Focus: /p/ and /k/ plosives

Unique New York, you know you need unique New York.

Focus: /j/ and /n/ with rhythm
Exercise 3

Shadowing in five steps

  1. Choose a clear recording of 30–60 seconds with a transcript.
  2. Listen once without reading, just to catch the melody.
  3. Read the transcript and mark stressed syllables and pauses.
  4. Play it again and speak along just behind the speaker, copying rhythm and pitch.
  5. Repeat daily for a week with the same clip, then choose a new one.

Shadowing is powerful because it trains the rhythm and intonation of whole phrases, not just single sounds.

Exercise 4

Stress in real sentences

In natural English, content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) are stressed and function words (a, the, to, of) are reduced. Read these sentences, stressing only the capitalised words:

  • I WANT to GO to the MARKET on SATURDAY.
  • She BOUGHT a NEW BOOK for her BROTHER.
  • CAN you SEND me the REPORT by FRIDAY?
  • We MET at a CAFÉ near the STATION.

Now change which word you stress in “I didn’t say she took the book”. Each version has a slightly different meaning. Try it!
